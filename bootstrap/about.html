Our story

The idea came from a simple annoyance. Students meet up to play something, and within ten minutes somebody is ordering a drink they do not want just to keep the table. An anti-cafe reverses that arrangement in a real building: you pay for time, and the tea is already included. This project asks whether the same arrangement works on a screen, with no building at all.

So the site sells one thing, time, and gives everything else away with it. Every game is open to every pass, and the cafe runs on points rather than a second payment. The treats are virtual on purpose. They cost nothing to make, they can be given to a friend in one click, and they turn a balance of points into something that feels like sitting in a cafe with people you like.

What comes next

This stage is HTML and CSS only, so the Play and Order buttons are placeholders and the points balance on the cafe page is a fixed number. The next stage adds JavaScript: single-player and same-device minigames, a points balance that really goes up when a round finishes and down when a treat is ordered, and the ability to send a treat to another account.

This copy of the site is built with Bootstrap 5.3 for Assignment 3. The main site stays plain HTML5 and one hand-written CSS file, and the next stages continue from there.
